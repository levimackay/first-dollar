A note before you sign up

Demo copy. This is where the founder's note goes, from COPY.md, in the founder's own words. The paragraphs below only stand in for its length.

Say who the product is for, in the plain terms a buyer would use about their own week. Name the job, the place it happens and the part of it that goes wrong today.

Say what the buyer does now instead. A spreadsheet, a phone call, a folder of paper, a person they pay by the hour. That workaround is the real competitor, and the reader knows it better than you do.

Say what happens after they pay: what arrives, when it arrives, and who answers when it does not work. Dates and names beat adjectives here.

Say what the deposit buys and how it comes back if the product does not ship. A buyer who can see the way out reads the rest of the page more calmly.

Say what is not built yet. A reader who finds the gap on their own stops trusting the parts that are finished, so name it first and say when it closes.

Say how many buyers you can take at once and why that number. A small first group is a fact about your time, and saying so plainly reads better than any countdown.

Say why you are building it. One concrete moment is enough: the afternoon the problem cost you or someone you work with real money or real time.

Close with the one thing you are asking for, in one sentence, and stop. The button below the note does the rest.
